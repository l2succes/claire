The Claire point of view

AI should be an instrument, not an impersonator. Claire helps people express what they mean with more clarity and consistency, while the user remains the author of every message and decision.

Audience

Product proof

Positioning

Production-ready concept: the unified inbox

Ready to produce · 45 sec

“You left them on read”

The broadest introduction to Claire: start with a familiar social mistake, reveal the fragmented inbox problem, then prove the unified inbox and assistant value.

Full script“Do you always forget to text your friends back? Or worse—do you think they never replied, then realize you’re the one who left them on read? Keeping up with messages across Instagram, WhatsApp, Telegram, and iMessage is exhausting. Claire brings all of those conversations into one place. You can see where every message came from, read it, and reply directly from one unified inbox. It also has an agent built in for the plans you said you’d remember—but forgot to put on your calendar. Claire can remind you, and even create the calendar invite. Personal or business, Claire helps you stay connected and follow through. Check it out.”
Shot list · 45 sec

Recording plan

  • 0–4 sec: Creator delivers the hook; cut to unread badges.
  • 4–8 sec: Animate Instagram, WhatsApp, Telegram, and iMessage around the phone.
  • 8–20 sec: Reveal and scroll Claire’s unified inbox; pause on platform badges.
  • 20–25 sec: Open a conversation and reply in place.
  • 25–38 sec: Show a forgotten plan becoming a reminder and calendar event.
  • 38–45 sec: Close on the creator, inbox montage, and Claire branding.
Motion noteBring each platform visual in as its name is spoken, then collapse all four toward a central Claire inbox. Avoid real usernames, private messages, and notification counts.

Content pillar: AI that helps you sound like yourself

Talking-head / 45–60 sec

“Are you speaking to AI—or with it?”

Explore the spectrum: people who feel uneasy using Gmail autocomplete, people who ask AI to write an entire email, and the middle ground—using it to sharpen your own thought without losing your voice.

Core line“I don’t want AI to dictate my voice. I want it to help me say what I already mean—more clearly, more often.”
Visual directionCreator types a rough message, Claire offers optional wording, and the creator makes the final edit before sending.
Cinematic reference / 30–45 sec

“Her, but grounded”

A playful discussion of the future people imagine when they think about AI companionship—then bring it back to what is useful today: context, reminders, draft support, and a second perspective.

Opening beat“When people ask if we’re talking to AI now, they’re usually imagining Her. We are not there—and honestly, that’s not the point.”
Visual directionUse an original, warm futuristic homage: a person walking with a discreet earbud and soft phone glow. Do not use footage or audio from the film.
Demo / 30 sec

Your voice, higher volume

Show the difference between “write this for me” and “help me make this clearer, warmer, and more direct.” Position Claire as a collaborator that lets users preserve intent while reducing communication friction.

Demo sequenceRough draft → two optional suggestions → user combines or edits them → final message retains their distinctive wording.
Founder POV / 45 sec

The communication economy

Make the larger argument: communication has become high-volume, fragmented, and consequential. The value of AI is not replacing connection; it is helping people have the bandwidth to maintain it.

Core line“The scarce thing isn’t care. It’s the time and mental space to turn care into a reply, a plan, or a follow-up.”

Content pillar: WhatsApp and life in Latin America

Founder story · 45–60 sec

“WhatsApp is the operating system of Latin America”

A founder perspective on how communication works in the region, and why the reality of many active chats across many apps led to Claire.

Full script“I spent the last six years of my life living in Latin America, and one thing I noticed right away is how much gets done on WhatsApp. Business conversations happen there. Personal conversations happen there. Group chats organize everything. WhatsApp really is the operating system of Latin America. But it also became hard for me to keep track of every group chat and conversation I was in. I had WhatsApp for business and personal life, Instagram, LinkedIn, and iMessage. I was always trying to remember where a conversation lived and who I still needed to get back to. That is why I built Claire. Claire organizes my conversations across all my apps in one place. And there is a personal AI assistant on top that reminds me when I need to follow up, so I do not accidentally leave people on read.”
Shot list · 45–60 sec

Recording plan

  • 0–8 sec: Creator on a street, in a café, or at a desk in Latin America. “I spent the last six years...”
  • 8–18 sec: Quick visual montage of business, a family chat, and lively group-chat activity represented by generic WhatsApp-style bubbles.
  • 18–30 sec: Show the clutter: WhatsApp, Instagram, LinkedIn, and iMessage, each with a different conversation.
  • 30–42 sec: Reveal Claire's unified inbox and show conversations organized together.
  • 42–60 sec: Show a follow-up reminder from the assistant, then finish on the founder speaking to camera.
On-screen caption hook“In Latin America, your life happens on WhatsApp. The problem is, the rest of it happens everywhere else.”

Content pillar: staying close after leaving home

Creator story / 45–60 sec

“When you leave home, staying close takes work”

A personal, unpretentious reflection on having people you care about in many places—and the quiet effort it takes to keep those relationships alive once you start traveling.

Full script“Have you ever left home, started traveling, and realized you suddenly have people you care about everywhere—but it’s harder than ever to stay in touch with them? Your people are in different time zones, and every relationship ends up living in a different app. I talk to my mom on WhatsApp. I talk to friends on Instagram. Telegram is where one of my work projects lives. And when everything is spread out like that, it’s really easy to let someone you care about slip through the cracks. That’s why I built Claire. It brings my conversations into one place, so I can actually stay on top of the people and commitments in my life. And I built an AI assistant on top of it that can nudge me when I forgot to reply or follow up. It has honestly changed my life—and that’s why I’m sharing it with you.”
Shot list / 45–60 sec

Recording plan

  • 0–7 sec: Creator walking through an airport, café, or unfamiliar street. “Have you ever left home, started traveling…”
  • 7–15 sec: Show a map / time zones / a contact list; then talking head.
  • 15–25 sec: Animate or film WhatsApp for Mom, Instagram for friends, and Telegram for work.
  • 25–36 sec: Show Claire’s unified inbox as the answer to scattered conversations.
  • 36–48 sec: Show an AI reminder or follow-up suggestion, then the creator responding.
  • 48–60 sec: Return to talking head and close with the personal payoff: “It has honestly changed my life.”
On-screen caption hook“When you leave home, the hard part isn’t meeting people. It’s staying close to them.”

Content pillar: remembering what you said you would do

Animated story · 45 sec

“Saturday plans, Sunday realization”

Dramatize the painful version first: you make plans for Saturday, wake up Sunday, and realize you forgot. Then rewind and show how Claire could have intervened the day before.

Core sequencePlans made in chat → Sunday-morning realization → rewind → Claire notification: “You made plans with Jamie tomorrow. Want to confirm?” → one tap creates a draft → user edits it → plans confirmed.
Closing line“Claire helps you remember the people—and the promises—that matter.”
Animation direction

Notification-to-draft flow

  • 0–12 sec: Contrast Saturday’s confident plan with Sunday’s forgotten commitment.
  • 12–17 sec: Claire reveal and visual rewind.
  • 17–24 sec: A notification bubble surfaces the plan one day early.
  • 24–34 sec: The user taps, receives a draft, and changes the final sentence.
  • 34–45 sec: Confirmation sent; resolve with Claire’s value proposition.
Product principleThe user always edits or approves the message. The assistant supplies context and momentum—not a replacement personality.

Content pillar: a useful second perspective in dating and relationships

Educational / 45–60 sec

“Better communication is not better pickup lines”

React to the wave of apps that help users with lines or dating-chat advice. Reframe the problem: people often need help slowing down, reading the context, and responding with honesty—not performing a personality.

GuardrailKeep it inclusive and non-creepy. Claire should never manipulate, deceive, diagnose, or claim to tell a user what another person thinks or feels.
Core line“A second perspective can help you be more considerate. It should never become a tool for pretending to be someone else.”
Product demo / 30 sec

“Before you send it”

Show an optional conversation check: a user asks Claire to identify the visible tone, point out what is unresolved, and offer a few authentic ways to respond. The user chooses and edits the reply.

Example prompts“What’s the tone here?” · “What am I missing?” · “Help me respond warmly without over-explaining.”
Product status: This is already substantially supported in Claire: Ask Claire can check conversation tone, draft replies, find open loops, and use configured tone/personality preferences. The client also has in-chat reply suggestions. No new feature ticket was created; the opportunity is packaging and marketing the existing capability responsibly.
Editorial rule:

Every video should make user control visible: suggestions are optional, context is treated carefully, and the person—not the model—writes the final message. Avoid claims that Claire can accurately infer hidden feelings, intentions, or compatibility from a conversation.
